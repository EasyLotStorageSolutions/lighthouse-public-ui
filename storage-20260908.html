1. Your space and start date

Your completed rental schedule identifies the legal operator, customer, selected lot, exact space, approved vehicle or property, access hours, start date and billing date. North Rose (110 North Rose Street, Baltimore, MD 21213) and Hollins Ferry (2619 Hollins Ferry Road, Baltimore, MD 21230) are separate facilities; a rental does not permit parking at both. Dimensions are nominal and maneuvering clearance must be confirmed. Only the assigned space may be used. No subletting, vehicle substitution or additional property without written approval. An availability request is not a reservation. Occupancy begins only after the completed agreement is accepted, required verification and initial payment clear, and the operator confirms the assignment.

2. Pricing, deposits and payments

Monthly rent is paid in advance. Motor-vehicle parking is currently $125 rent plus $25 Baltimore City parking tax for 10×20; $175 plus $35 for 10×30; and $225 plus $45 for 10×40. Monthly totals are $150, $210 and $270. Other uses require a written tax classification and quote. The standard draft has no security deposit, setup fee, late fee or returned-payment fee. Any different charge must be specifically priced and accepted in the completed schedule before occupancy; no unspecified charge is authorized. Actual, reasonable and documented damage or cleanup costs caused by the customer or their permitted visitors may be invoiced to the extent allowed by law. An invoice is not permission to charge a saved card for those costs. Failed payment does not erase rent already owed. Billing disputes may be raised through the account support form or 443-901-3279 without waiving legal dispute rights.

3. Renewal, autopay and cancellation

The completed schedule must choose a fixed one-month term or a monthly renewing term. A request for availability enrolls you in neither. Automatic renewal and recurring charges require separate, unselected consent showing the amount, frequency, first charge date and cancellation method. For a renewing agreement, cancel before the next renewal to avoid the next term; no extra advance-notice period or cancellation fee applies under this standard draft. Cancellation of recurring charges takes effect when received and does not require staff approval. It does not erase an existing balance. The current paid term remains available through its stated end date. Early departure ordinarily does not prorate an already-started month, except when law requires a refund or the operator cannot provide the agreed space. Duplicate, unauthorized and incorrectly calculated charges will be corrected. The completed agreement must supply a tested online cancellation route and an alternative contact method before automatic renewal is offered. A move-out review request alone is not that route. Rate changes require at least 30 days' advance written notice and a chance to cancel before the increased rate takes effect; no retroactive increase applies.

4. Identity, ownership and insurance

The customer must be an adult with authority to enter the agreement and must provide accurate contact and mailing details. Before approval, supply proof of lawful ownership or the owner's written permission, identifying information for the stored vehicle or property, and any lienholder details relevant to lawful notices. Keep required registration and insurance current. Maintain insurance appropriate to the stored property, including theft, weather and physical-damage coverage where available, and legally required liability insurance for vehicles operated at the lot. The operator's insurance does not insure customer property. Provide evidence of coverage before move-in and on reasonable request, and report a lapse promptly. Do not place identity documents, insurance records, payment details or gate codes in public forms or public photo links.

5. Lot rules and prohibited use

Use is limited to the approved parking or storage purpose. No residence, sleeping, camping, retail activity, vehicle repair, washing, dumping, charging from unapproved electrical sources, or storage outside the assigned boundaries. No stolen or unlawful property, explosives, hazardous waste or leaking vehicles. Ordinary fuel in a vehicle's installed tank is permitted only as allowed by applicable fire rules; separate fuel containers require written approval and legal compliance. Keep traffic lanes, gates, emergency access and neighboring spaces clear. Drive slowly and follow posted rules. Supervise children and visitors. Do not tamper with cameras, fences, lighting or locks. Report spills and hazards promptly; the customer is responsible for reasonable documented remediation costs caused by their breach, subject to law.

6. Personal access and arrival

Gate access is personal to approved users. Do not share a PIN publicly, lend it to an unapproved user, allow tailgating or force the gate. Secure the gate after use according to the arrival instructions. Report lost or compromised access promptly. The igloohome Smart Padlock 2 instructions and assigned-space photograph are released after the operator verifies the agreement, payment and assignment. A photo identifies a space; it is not proof of uninterrupted surveillance or insurance. Access hours are those in the completed schedule; 24-hour access is not promised. Temporary restrictions may be needed for a genuine safety emergency or necessary maintenance, with reasonable notice where practicable and a lawful retrieval arrangement. Payment problems do not trigger an automatic lockout through this portal.

7. Property care and security expectations

This is outdoor, self-service parking or storage. The customer retains possession and control of the property and ordinarily keeps the keys. The parties intend a space rental rather than custodial safekeeping; actual conduct and applicable law govern that relationship. No valet, maintenance, weather protection or guarantee against theft, vandalism, flood, fire, pests or other loss is included. Locks, lighting and any installed cameras reduce some risks but do not guarantee prevention, continuous monitoring or complete recordings. Lock and secure the property, remove valuables, inspect its condition and report problems promptly. Nothing here excuses the operator from duties or liability that cannot lawfully be excluded.

8. Responsibility for damage and third-party claims

Each party remains responsible to the extent required by law for harm caused by its own acts or omissions. To the extent legally permitted, the customer will indemnify the operator for third-party claims, reasonable defense costs and damage to the extent caused by the customer's breach, unlawful conduct, negligence, or the conduct of visitors the customer authorizes. This does not cover harm caused by the operator's own negligence, gross negligence, intentional misconduct or violation of a nonwaivable duty. The operator must promptly notify the customer of a covered claim and reasonably cooperate; no settlement may impose an admission or nonmonetary duty on the customer without consent. Insurance requirements do not enlarge this indemnity. No clause waives mandatory consumer remedies.

9. Default, termination and lawful enforcement

Nonpayment, material misrepresentation, unauthorized property and material violations of these rules are defaults. The operator may demand cure, decline an additional term, or terminate as permitted by the agreement and law, with any required notice. Immediate safety measures must be proportionate to an actual hazard. Property is not automatically abandoned because rent is late, a card fails or the customer stops responding. The operator will use only remedies legally applicable to the facility and circumstances, including required notices and protections for owners, lienholders and servicemembers. No automatic sale, forfeiture, tow, title transfer or disposal is authorized by an online request. Any access restriction or enforcement action requires individual review and lawful procedures.

IF MARYLAND’S SELF-SERVICE STORAGE ACT APPLIES: The operator has a statutory lien on property in the rented space for amounts covered by law. Property may be sold to satisfy that lien following default and all required procedures. A motor vehicle or watercraft may be towed or removed after default for more than 60 days, subject to applicable law. A lien sale will be advertised in a newspaper of general circulation in the jurisdiction, by electronic mail, or on an online website as permitted by law. These provisions do not authorize immediate towing, sale or disposal.

10. Move-out and condition records

Remove all approved property and rubbish by the agreed end date, leave the space reasonably clean, and report completion with a dated photograph if practicable. The operator will record the condition and confirm receipt; an administrative delay in inspection does not restart a canceled renewal or authorize another recurring charge. Stopping autopay does not physically surrender the space. If property remains, lawful occupancy charges and remedies may apply, but no punitive multiplier or unapproved saved-card charge is created by this clause. Any claimed damage or cleanup amount must be itemized with reasonable supporting evidence. Existing balances and accrued claims survive termination; access credentials are retired after lawful surrender or termination.

11. Records, privacy and communications

The operator uses contact, vehicle, agreement, payment-status and access information to administer the rental, resolve incidents and satisfy legal obligations. Payments are handled through the identified payment provider; do not send card numbers or security codes to staff. Rental documents and access details belong in the private customer account. Any installed security cameras are for property operations and incident review; this clause does not assert that cameras exist or record continuously at either lot. Records should be accessible only to authorized personnel and service providers, retained for a documented operational or legal purpose, and securely removed when no longer needed, subject to legal holds. Marketing consent is optional and separate. Provide current email and postal contact details and promptly report changes. Statutory notices follow the methods required by law, even if ordinary rental communications are electronic.

12. Agreement, changes and disputes

The completed schedule, these versioned terms, any required statutory addendum and separately accepted payment authorization form the rental agreement. The signed schedule controls a specific price or service detail, subject to law. Oral promises do not change the written agreement unless confirmed in writing by an authorized operator representative. Material changes require advance notice and any consent required by law. Maryland law governs, subject to controlling federal law. Disputes may be brought in a court with lawful jurisdiction; statutory venue and small-claims rights remain intact. No forced arbitration, jury waiver or class-action waiver is imposed by this draft. An invalid provision is limited or severed only to the extent law permits, leaving the remainder effective where possible. The customer receives a retainable copy of the completed agreement and all consents.
